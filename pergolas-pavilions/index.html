Timber frame pergolas & pavilions

Solid 7×7 and 8×8 Douglas Fir, cut and fitted by hand and pinned with oak. We build pergolas over decks, pavilions over hot tubs, and the occasional gazebo, greenhouse or shed.

The difference

You can buy a kit from a big-box store. Those are bolted together, built from smaller lumber, or made with built-up posts designed to look like solid timber. Standing next to the real thing, it is not close.

Ours are solid timber, joined the way frames have been joined for thousands of years. A tenon is cut on one timber, a mortise is cut in the other, and an oak peg is driven through both.

The peg holes are drilled slightly out of line on purpose. That is drawboring. As the peg goes in, it drags the tenon deeper into the mortise and locks the joint tight with no metal involved.

Questions

Before you ask

What are your pergolas made of?

Solid Douglas Fir timbers, typically 7×7 or 8×8, joined with mortise and tenon and pinned with drawbored oak pegs. No built-up posts and no bolted-together kits.

Do I need a permit for a pergola in the Denver area?

In most Denver suburbs, a structure under about 200 square feet (roughly 14×14) does not need a permit. Larger structures may. Rules differ by city. If yours needs a permit, we handle it.

Can you build over a hot tub or a deck?

Yes. The Erie pavilion covers a hot tub with room left for chairs, and the Golden pergola sits on a cedar deck we built first. The structure is sized to what is under it.

Can you design something custom?

Yes. Every build is modeled in 3D before we cut, so a custom design goes through the same process as a standard one. We also have several designs ready to go that we can adjust to fit your space.

Do you build gazebos, greenhouses and sheds?

Yes. Pergolas and pavilions are most of what we do, but the same timber frame approach works for gazebos, greenhouses and sheds.
