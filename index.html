The frame

What goes into a Log Dog build

A kit from a big-box store looks like timber framing from the driveway. Up close it is smaller lumber and a lot of hardware.

Timber
Solid 7×7 or 8×8 Douglas Fir posts and beams. Cedar or Douglas Fir for arbors and trellises.
Joinery
Mortise and tenon, laid out and cut by hand.
Fastening
Drawbored oak pegs instead of bolts and screws. The peg holes are offset on purpose, so driving the peg pulls the joint tight.
Roof
Open rafters over a deck, or a full roof of tongue-and-groove decking under metal.
Design
Modeled in 3D, down to the peg holes, before a single timber is cut.
Permits
Most Denver suburbs do not require a permit under about 200 square feet (roughly 14×14). If yours does, we handle it.
Where
The Denver metro, and north along the Front Range.

The process

A model first, then the timber

  1. I

    Walk the yard

    We visit with a tape measure, not a template. Where the sun sits at 6 pm in July, where snow drifts in January and what the frame goes over all change the footprint.

  2. II

    Model it

    Every post, beam, brace and rafter is built in 3D at real dimensions, down to the peg holes. That gives us a cut list, so nothing is sized by eye on site.

  3. III

    Cut it by hand

    Layout goes onto the timbers at full scale. Lines get snapped, joints get scribed, and the mortises and tenons are cut with chisels and saws.

  4. IV

    Raise it

    Posts go up, beams go on, pegs get driven. Every joint fits because we already knew it would.
